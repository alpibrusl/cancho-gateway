The refusal lab

Type any HTTP request head — hostile, malformed, smuggled, or perfectly ordinary — and the gateway’s framing judge answers in your browser: accept, refuse with a rule tag, or read more. No server, no binary download: the judge is the gateway’s own decision code, ported byte for byte, and it proves on every load that it agrees with what the real probe answered.

The corpus, agreed in advance

The smuggling corpus of tests/smuggling/corpus.py — RFC 9112 and Kettle 2019 cases — was run through the real probe at the pinned compiler, and its 57 answers recorded in docs/lab-corpus.json (regenerated and checked in CI). On every load this page re-judges all of them and shows the count above; a disagreement would show here in red, and the lab’s claim would be dead.

The three head-limit cases (16 KiB of padding) are constructed by the corpus runner, not carried in the file; the lab does not replay them.

What this is, honestly

  • The judge is the gateway’s decision code, ported — std.http.parse and framing.judge, the same branches in the same order — not a re-imagining. It is verified against the probe’s recorded answers, which is a strong check, not a proof of equivalence on every input.
  • Nothing here runs the binary. No socket, no gateway process, no authority report is involved in your browser session. The demo binary is in the release; the framing rules are evidenced here.
  • The lab judges heads, not routes. A head that is accepted here still has to find a route, a method and an upstream — those refusals (route.none, limit.body, and the rest) are the proxy’s, and they need the binary.
  • Request ids are elided (…): a real one is issued per connection by the binary.
